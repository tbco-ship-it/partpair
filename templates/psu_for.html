{{ gpu.short }} power supply requirements

The {{ gpu.name }} is rated at {{ gpu.tdp }} W board power. Short transient spikes can reach about {{ (gpu.tdp * 1.5)|round|int }} W, so the power supply must have headroom beyond the sum of the parts. Here is what that means with three classes of processor.

How we calculate it

Estimated load = CPU maximum turbo power + {{ gpu.tdp }} W for the card + 100 W for motherboard, memory, storage and fans. We multiply by 1.3 for transient headroom and round up to the next common PSU size. Full details on the methodology page.

Connectors

{% if gpu.tdp >= 300 %}Cards in this power class use the 12V-2x6 (12VHPWR) connector or three 8-pin PCIe connectors depending on the board partner. Prefer an ATX 3.x power supply with a native 12V-2x6 cable over adapters.{% elif gpu.tdp >= 200 %}Expect two 8-pin PCIe connectors or a single 12V-2x6 depending on the model. Most 650–850 W units from the last five years have both.{% else %}A single 8-pin (or 6+2-pin) PCIe connector is typical; some low-power models draw everything from the slot. Almost any modern PSU qualifies.{% endif %}

When to step up a tier

Heavy overclocking, custom water cooling, more than four drives or a lot of RGB each add tens of watts. If two or more apply, buy the next size up. Efficiency and fan noise are also best at 50–70% load, which the 1.3× rule already lands you in.
